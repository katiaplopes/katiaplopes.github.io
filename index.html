Computational genomics of the aging brain

I study how genes are regulated in the human brain and how those patterns change in Alzheimer’s disease. I use human genetics, single-cell genomics, and multi-omics to connect molecular changes to brain pathology.

Research directions

  • Map genetic effects in brain cell populations Combine genetic association and colocalization with single-cell and multi-omic data to identify where disease-associated variants may influence gene regulation.
  • Connect molecular changes to disease progression Relate cell-type-specific molecular networks to neuropathology and measures of cognitive decline.
  • Model molecular systems across brain cell types Build gene co-expression networks and integrate transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data to investigate how coordinated molecular changes relate to Alzheimer’s disease.

Teaching and mentorship

I teach systems biology in the Health Sciences Program at Institute of Medical Assistance to State Civil Servants (IAMSPE) and mentor graduate researchers at IAMSPE and the Federal University of Paraná. Recent projects have explored cytokine expression in the aging brain, microglial communication, and Mendelian randomization.
